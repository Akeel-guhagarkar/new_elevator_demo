01 — Overview

A complete controller, running in the browser

The demonstration recreates the hardware training model: a miniature glass-shaft elevator driven by a geared DC motor and controlled by a PLC, with an HMI touchscreen and a physical operator panel. Each part of the model has a working counterpart on screen.

02 — Key features

Built like a real elevator controller

Eight features that mirror how a real PLC-controlled elevator behaves, from safety interlocks to live monitoring.

Control

Collective control

Calls are served in the direction of travel. Opposite requests wait, and the cabin reverses only when nothing is left ahead.

Safety

Door interlock

The motor cannot start until the door-closed limit switch confirms the door is fully shut.

Modes

Auto and maintenance

Run normally in auto, or switch to maintenance and jog the cabin by hand with UP and DOWN.

Safety

Emergency stop

A latched stop halts the system, sounds the alarm buzzer and blocks new calls until it is released.

Interface

HMI touchscreen

Dashboard, I/O monitor, faults, maintenance, history and speed settings on one screen.

Logic

Live ladder diagram

Watch each rung light up in real time as you press buttons and the cabin moves.

I/O

PLC-style addressing

Inputs, outputs, relays, timers and a counter use X, Y, M, T and C addresses with live status.

Feedback

Sound and voice

Arrival chimes, door and motor sounds, and spoken floor announcements.

03 — How it works

From a button press to a stopped cabin

01

Request

Calls come from inside the cabin, or from the UP and DOWN stations at each floor.

02

Decide

Requests are served in the direction of travel. Opposite calls are skipped, and the cabin reverses when nothing is left ahead.

03

Move

The door must be fully closed before the motor starts. The cabin accelerates, cruises and brakes at each stop.

04

Monitor

The HMI, the internal display and the PLC views show floor, direction, door state, timers and every I/O point.
